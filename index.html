Cascadia Estates: King County housing market report

Where, when and what kind of homes sell in King County, and which deals fall outside the normal price range?

A seven-page interactive report built for the owner of a real-estate brokerage. It covers about 4,600 home sales from May to July 2014 and lets the owner move from a market overview down to single cities, property features and unusual deals.

  • Star schema with a Sales fact table and Date and Location dimensions, plus a Region to City drill-down hierarchy.
  • Advanced DAX: IQR outlier detection, TOPN rankings, an SVG sales-calendar heatmap and a linear-trend forecast four weeks ahead.
  • Bookmark tabs that switch the price view between all homes, standard deals and outliers, a custom tooltip page, and cross-filtering between visuals.
  • A Process Notes page that documents the data, cleaning, model and every design decision.
Report pages
  1. About the Project. Topic and research questions.
  2. Market Overview. KPIs, homes by region with drill-down, weekly trend and forecast, price bands.
  3. Location & Price. City scatter on a log scale against the county median, top 10 cities.
  4. Property Drivers. Waterfront, view and renovation premiums, size bands and bedrooms.
  5. Time & Activity. Weekday by week heatmap, busiest weekdays, daily sales pace by month.
  6. Price Landscape. Price distribution with tabs for standard deals and outliers by city.
  7. Process Notes. Data, cleaning, model, analysis and key conclusions.

What drives home prices in King County?

Which physical and location features are most strongly related to home prices?

Exploratory analysis of 4,600 home sales and 18 columns in a Jupyter notebook. Every cleaning decision is explained, and each chart ends with a written conclusion.

0.70correlation between living area and price, the strongest single factor
4.3×Medina's median price compared with Seattle's
34%of all sales are in Seattle, yet it ranks 18th of 44 cities on median price
  • Cleaned the data with documented rules: removed 55 rows (1.2%) with $0 prices or extreme values, and fixed rows where build and renovation years were swapped instead of deleting them.
  • Showed that size alone does not explain the price gap between cities. Living area relates to price almost equally in Seattle (0.75) and elsewhere (0.73), so location adds a premium on top.
  • Found that busier weeks had lower median prices (r = −0.75 over 9 full weeks), most likely because more lower-priced homes sold in those weeks.
  • Turned the results into practical advice for buyers, sellers and agents.

Wide World Importers: revenue, customers and churn risk

How is the business growing, who are the customers that matter most, and where is the risk?

Ten business questions answered with T-SQL on Microsoft's WideWorldImporters database, a wholesale company with sales, purchasing and warehouse tables.

  • Measured yearly revenue growth with an annualized run rate, so partial years compare fairly (CTEs and LAG).
  • Flagged potential churn: customers whose time since their last order is more than twice their usual gap between orders.
  • Ranked the top customers per quarter and the top products by revenue (DENSE_RANK, TOP).
  • Found the main business risk: two chains, Wingtip Toys and Tailspin Toys, hold 459 of 663 stores (69%). Losing either one would cut the customer base by tens of percent.
All 10 questions
  1. Yearly growth. Revenue per year, annualized, with growth rate.
  2. Top customers. Five highest-revenue customers in every quarter.
  3. Best-selling products. Top 10 items by total sales value.
  4. Product margin. Ranking items by retail price minus unit price.
  5. Supplier catalog. Each supplier with its product list (STRING_AGG).
  6. Top customers by geography. City, country, continent and region.
  7. Monthly sales. Monthly totals, yearly subtotals and running totals (ROLLUP).
  8. Order seasonality. Orders per month across 2013 to 2016 (PIVOT).
  9. Churn risk. Days between orders against days since the last order.
  10. Customer concentration. Customer share per category, counting each chain once.
